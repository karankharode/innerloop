# Name and domain options

Five candidates, each with the reasoning and the honest downside. The codebase
currently ships as **Innerloop** — changing it is a one-line edit in
`lib/brand.ts` plus the copy in `docs/LAUNCH-COPY.md`.

> Domain availability changes daily and I have not checked any registrar.
> Check each one before you commit, and check the handle on X, Instagram and
> LinkedIn at the same time — a free domain with a taken handle is a headache.

---

## 1. Innerloop  ·  `innerloop.app` / `innerloop.so` / `getinnerloop.com`

**Why it works.** "Inner loop" is the tight feedback cycle you run before
anything ships — exactly what the product is, and a phrase your build-in-public
audience already uses about their own work. The double meaning (inner life /
inner loop) does the positioning for you without being precious about it.

**The downside.** It is a common enough compound that the .com is likely gone
and there is dev-tooling noise in search results. You would be competing for
the term with build systems.

**Best if** you want the product to read as a tool for people who build things,
not as a wellness app.

---

## 2. Plumbline  ·  `plumbline.app` / `plumbline.co`

**Why it works.** A plumb line is the oldest instrument for checking whether
something is true and upright. Concrete, physical, unhurried — the opposite of
app-store self-help. Gives you an obvious visual mark (a hanging weight) and a
natural verb: "run a plumbline".

**The downside.** Some people read "plumbing" first. It is also a word people
have to hear once before it lands, which costs you a beat in a 10-second pitch.

**Best if** you want the brand to feel like an instrument rather than a service.

---

## 3. Fieldnote  ·  `fieldnote.app` / `fieldnotes.so`

**Why it works.** Field notes are what you write while the observation is still
fresh — descriptive, not conclusive. Sets the exact expectation you want: this
records what you said, it does not diagnose you. Pluralises well for the
history feature ("your field notes").

**The downside.** Crowded. Several note-taking and journalling products already
live near this word, and you would be the smallest of them.

**Best if** you plan to lean into the history/archive side of the product more
than the session side.

---

## 4. Northing  ·  `northing.app` / `northing.co`

**Why it works.** A real surveying term — the north-south component of a
position. Sounds like "north" without the exhausted "north star" cliché, and it
implies a coordinate rather than a destination, which is more honest about what
ten questions can do. Short, one word, easy to say.

**The downside.** Slightly obscure. Also risks sounding like "nothing" when
someone hears it in a noisy room or a podcast ad.

**Best if** you want something short and ownable and are willing to teach the
word.

---

## 5. Quietloop  ·  `quietloop.app` / `quietloop.com`

**Why it works.** Names the feeling rather than the mechanism: the reflective
loop you run when nothing is shouting at you. Pairs naturally with the
no-feed, no-streak, no-notification stance, which is a genuine differentiator
worth putting in the name.

**The downside.** "Quiet" skews soft and could pull the product toward the
meditation-app shelf, which is exactly the adjacency you said you do not want.

**Best if** the anti-app positioning is the main wedge.

---

## Recommendation

**Innerloop**, with **Plumbline** as the fallback if the domain situation is
bad. Innerloop is the only one of the five that speaks directly to the audience
you are launching to — founders and builders — while still being honest about
what the product does. Plumbline is the more distinctive long-term brand but
needs more explaining, which costs you on a cold launch post.
